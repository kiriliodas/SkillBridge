# Study Design

Use to plan learning a skill or a body of knowledge under a real time constraint, or to help
someone who is studying inefficiently.

## Start from the target, not the syllabus

- **What must the person be able to do** by the end? "Configure a CI pipeline" and "understand CI" imply different plans.
- **How will that be tested?** A certification exam, a work task, an interview. The assessment determines the practice.
- **What is the deadline, and how many hours per week actually exist?** Plans that assume ideal weeks fail in week two.

Work backwards from the assessment. A syllabus read linearly is the slowest possible route to
being able to perform a task.

## Study methods, ranked by evidence

1. **Retrieval practice** — close the book and recall, or answer a question, before re-reading. The single highest-yield technique, and the least pleasant.
2. **Spaced repetition** — revisit at increasing intervals instead of cramming. Necessary for anything that must be retained beyond the exam.
3. **Interleaving** — mix problem types rather than drilling one kind in a block. Harder during practice, better at transfer.
4. **Elaboration** — explain it in your own words, or teach it; gaps in understanding surface immediately.
5. **Worked examples** — for genuinely new material, study a worked solution before attempting problems.
6. **Re-reading and highlighting** — feel productive, produce almost nothing. Use only to locate material, never to learn it.

## The weekly structure that works

- **A short diagnostic first**: attempt the target task, badly. It shows exactly what is missing and makes the learning concrete.
- **60–70% practice, 30–40% input.** Most study plans invert this and become comfortable reading.
- **One artefact per week** — a working script, a written summary from memory, a solved problem set. Having produced something is the difference between having read and having learned.
- **A review slot** for what was learned two and four weeks ago, not only this week's material.

## Diagnosing a stalled learner

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reads a lot, cannot do it | No retrieval practice | Replace re-reading with closed-book problems |
| Understands in the lesson, blank alone | Fluency illusion | Practice under exam conditions, without notes |
| Strong on drills, weak on new cases | Blocked practice | Interleave problem types |
| Forgets within two weeks | No spacing | Schedule reviews, shorten the initial block |
| Avoids the hard chapter | The prerequisite is missing | Diagnose upstream, do not push through |

## Planning the first two weeks

Be concrete: for each session, the topic, the practice material, and the check that proves it
stuck. "Study Chapter 3" is not a plan; "Do problems 3.4–3.9 closed-book, then re-do the two
missed ones in three days" is.

## Honesty about the plan

- A plan that requires heroic hours is a plan that will be abandoned. Budget the hours the person actually has.
- If the target is not reachable in the available time, say so and propose the reduced target. A learner who discovers this in week three loses the time entirely.
