# Meeting Decisions

Use to prepare, run or summarise a working meeting where something must be decided or
unblocked.

## Only meet when a meeting is the right instrument

A meeting is justified when you need synchronous judgement from people who cannot be
replaced by a document: a disagreement to resolve, a decision with several owners, an
unblocking that requires negotiation.

It is not justified for: a status update (write it), information distribution (record it),
or a decision one person is empowered to take (let them take it).

## Before: the agenda is a decision list

Send, in advance:

- **The decisions to be taken**, phrased as questions with an owner each. "Approve the migration plan for Q1" — not "migration".
- **The input each attendee must bring** — a document, a number, a position.
- **Who decides** for each item, and who is only consulted.
- **The time box per item.**

An agenda that lists topics rather than decisions produces discussion, not outcomes.

## During: protect the decision

- State the decision to be taken at the start of each item, and the deadline.
- Separate the three phases explicitly: **information** (facts, no debate), **options** (alternatives, no advocacy), **decision** (choose, then stop). Mixing them is what makes meetings long.
- When two people disagree, force the disagreement to a testable claim: what evidence would settle it? If none exists, it is a preference, and the owner decides.
- Park anything that needs more than its time box: add it to a list with an owner, and move on. Parking is not dropping.
- End each item by restating the decision in one sentence and confirming the owner heard it.

## After: the record that matters

Within the same day, send a short record:

```
Decisions
1. <decision> — owner, date. Rejected: <alternative> because <reason>.

Actions
- <action> — <owner>, due <date>.

Open questions
- <question> — who will resolve it, by when.
```

Decisions, actions and open questions. Not a transcript, not a summary of who said what.

## Why the rejected option matters

Recording why an alternative was rejected is the single highest-value line in the record. It
prevents the same option from returning in three weeks and lets a future reader understand
whether circumstances have changed enough to reopen it.

## Failure modes

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No decision at the end | Agenda listed topics | Rebuild around questions |
| Decision reversed next week | Decision-maker absent or unstated | Name the decider in advance |
| Same topic every week | No record of the previous decision | Keep the decision log |
| Everyone agrees, nothing changes | No owner, no date | Every action gets both |
| Half the room silent | Discussion between two people | Round-robin the input phase |

## The decision log

Keep a running document per team: date, decision, context in one line, owner, and the trigger
that would reopen it. It is the cheapest institutional memory a team can maintain, and it is
what makes a re-decision an informed choice rather than a re-litigation.
